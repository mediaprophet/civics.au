In plain terms / The cooperative

A knowledge bank, run like a credit union.

A knowledge bank cooperative is a neighbourhood institution for the digital age: it keeps a community’s information in the community, under the community’s own laws, with the people who run it bound to serve the members—never the other way round.

The cooperative runs the shared parts

A credit union pools a town's savings so the town benefits instead of a distant bank. A knowledge bank cooperative does the same for information: it runs the shared machinery—storage, connections, software updates, and data safes for people who do not want a box at home—while each member keeps authority over their own records. Members, not customers.

A bank vault, for information

The name is the metaphor: a bank does not just hold money—it keeps it in a vault, and it rents safe deposit boxes for the things people cannot afford to lose. A knowledge bank does the same for information. The vault service it provides is for natural persons—citizens and community members—not just organisations: safekeeping, availability and governed exchange of what each person holds.

And the bank's role stops at the door of the box. A bank holds your safe deposit box, but the contents are yours—providing the vault does not make the cooperative the owner of what is inside it. The person remains the principal; the cooperative is the fiduciary agent; the software is a sub-agent. Providing the service changes none of that.

Why "knowledge", not just data

Three words get blurred together: data is raw bytes—values, files, signals. Information is data organised into something meaningful—records, documents, statements. Knowledge is the layer on top: the logic that lets information be used—shared meanings, the rules exchanges run under, the ability to reason over it and reach sound conclusions.

The cooperative is a knowledge bank because it curates that top layer as well as safeguarding the rest: the shared dictionaries, the rules, the provenance that lets information be trusted and reasoned over—not merely warehoused. A data vault keeps bytes safe; a knowledge bank keeps them useful and truthful.

People who owe you a duty

The professionals who maintain the cooperative act under what the design calls an informatics fiduciary duty—the same kind of obligation a lawyer owes a client or a doctor owes a patient: strict loyalty to your interests, confidentiality, and no profiting from what they hold for you. It is a duty that can be enforced, not a slogan. And the software they run for you is a sub-agent—an obedient tool executing instructions, never an autonomous decision-maker.

When the box is lost—or when you are

A box at home can fail, be stolen, or become more than someone can afford to run. So the cooperative maintains datacentre services for encrypted backups and continuity: if a device is lost or stolen, the records survive—still encrypted, still under the owner's keys, still the owner's.

The same service supports choices most systems never offer: standing instructions for a person's records in the event of their death—what passes to family, what transfers to an executor, what is sealed or destroyed. A will for the digital estate, administered the same way everything else here is: by the person's own declared instructions, under local law.

Local law, not foreign fine print

Most services Australians use are governed by contracts written under foreign law—very often the courts of California, or wherever the company's head office sits. If something goes wrong, your remedy is overseas: expensive, slow, and in practice out of reach. When your records sit on a box in your town, held by a cooperative in your region, your rights are the laws of your own country—and your own courts can hear a complaint, including breaches of rights.

Local law also answers a quieter problem: a dispute is only as good as the evidence behind it. Records held about people today are rarely kept in a form a court could rely on—unsigned, unprovenanced, quietly alterable. The cooperative's records are kept evidence-grade: signed, timestamped and provenance-tracked, so a claim can be proven rather than merely asserted.
