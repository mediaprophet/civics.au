Opportunities / The social web

A social web, not social media.

Social media taught a generation that being social online means joining a global platform that watches you. The older, better idea is the reverse: social functions built into the web itself—relationships and conversations held in your own safe, communities running their own spaces under their own rules.

Intentions, assumptions & sources ↗

Built into the web, not owned by it

"The social web" was part of the original Web 3.0 vision—before the term was captured by crypto—describing relationships, sharing and messaging as functions of the web's protocols rather than a company's product (Sayre, 2010 ↗; the social web and the web of data ↗). In this design your social graph lives in your data safe and conversations move through governed exchange: no single platform holds everyone's social life—or gets to sell it.

Rooms with rules, not one global hall

Communities define their own social spaces: a school's parent group, a sporting club, a neighbourhood, an association. Each sets its own membership and norms, interoperating through shared standards—without a global gatekeeper, and without an engagement algorithm deciding what you see by what outrages you.

Safe places for the people who need them most

The capability that was missing a decade ago now exists: guardianship semantics enforced by a reasoning engine—rules applied exactly, not probabilistically. A parent or trusted guardian declares the boundaries; the engine enforces them deterministically; the child interacts with friends and family inside a local space that is structurally invisible to the global open web. Not content filtered after the fact—an environment where the unsafe was never reachable.

Follow how one boundary works:

Scoped authority

The rules are declared, not hidden

A parent or guardian sets the boundary—who the child can contact, what spaces they can enter, what can be shared—as an explicit, reviewable guardianship relation, not a settings page buried in a platform.

Deterministic

Rules, not vibes

A local reasoning engine applies the declared rules exactly—no probabilistic guessing, no engagement algorithm, no drift. What is outside the boundary is not "unlikely to appear"; it does not exist in the child's space.

Local by default

Friends and family, locally

The child chats, shares and plays with people in the community's own social space—reachable by the people the boundary permits, invisible to the global networks and their problems.

Visible & revocable

Guardianship leaves a record

Every act under the guardianship is recorded as the guardian acting for the child—visible, auditable and revocable. As the child grows, the boundaries shrink; guardianship is a scaffold, not a cage.

And your own courts, if it goes wrong

Because the social space runs on local infrastructure under local terms, a harm or dispute lands where the people live—Australian courts, Australian privacy principles—not behind a foreign choice-of-law clause pointing at California. The same jurisdictional protection described in the cooperative applies here with even higher stakes: the safety of children is not something to litigate overseas.

What it stands on

A safer social web is an application of everything beneath it: data safes holding people's social context, the databox governing exchange between members, guardianship relations for those who need protection, reasoning engines enforcing declared boundaries, and the knowledge-bank cooperative running the shared space. None of it requires trusting a global platform—because there isn't one.
