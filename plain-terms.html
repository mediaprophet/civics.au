The whole idea in one breath

More and more of daily life—banking, health, government services, work—runs through computers. But the computers, and the information inside them, mostly belong to distant companies under rules written far away. This project proposes the local alternative: a decent place to stay for people in transition, and community-owned infrastructure that lets a town keep its own information, run its own services, and deal with the wider world on fair terms.

Who wrote this, and why

This project and this site are the work of Timothy Holborn, an Australian who has spent years helping define many of the technology standards this project relies on—work done with very few others internationally, unpaid, funded at his own expense and through the support of those who kept him going while he did it. The site exists to pass that same structural support forward.

In 2011, Timothy was asked to help ensure Australian children were given an opportunity to better understand the nature of Australian civics; the short film The Australian Way was part of the materials made for that purpose.

The effort has continued since—to help ensure the sentiments it expresses are not mere fictions.

What a vote really is

The film talks about a vote. Most people hear that as an election—a ballot paper every few years. But a vote, more deeply, is every meaningful choice a person makes: the decisions they take, the path they choose, where their effort goes. Each day's effort is a ballot cast for the kind of world you want to live in—and this project is built to make those ballots count.

That is what the machinery is for. When a person chooses to give time to a cooperative project, that choice is a vote—logged, recognised, and repaid if the work earns. The record of those votes lives in the person's own safe, shown only when they choose to show it. And the grounds exist because choice itself needs a foundation: a person in survival mode has no real vote, because every decision is forced. A stable place to live restores the space in which a person can choose at all.

The purpose, then, is human before it is technical: that every person has the choice, the opportunity, the vote—the means to live an honourable life with dignity, and to shape better outcomes for themselves, their children and their communities.

The answer, in pieces

00A place to land

Before anything digital matters: a powered, connected, affordable community ground—for people who cannot currently carry the costs of a traditional home while they work to improve their situation, and for travellers who need little more than a hot shower and a safe place to park.

01A data safe of your own

Your records live on a small device you control—yours, under your rules. The technical word is a "pod"; the everyday word is a safe.

02A governed exchange

Organisations keep their own vaults too, and the two sides exchange records under agreed rules—with signed receipts both ways. That is the "databox".

03A network of cooperatives to run it

Local people maintain the shared machinery, the way a credit union keeps a town's money local—each community running its own "knowledge bank", interoperating as a network rather than one central body.

What we would ask of you

  • Read it. These pages are written to be questioned, not admired.
  • Say what is missing. Lived experience is evidence here—if the proposal overlooks something real, that is the most useful thing you can tell us.
  • Start a conversation. If you lead a community organisation, council or club, a discussion costs nothing and teaches both sides.
  • Consider a pilot. If a real site interests you, the expressions of interest page is the next step.
