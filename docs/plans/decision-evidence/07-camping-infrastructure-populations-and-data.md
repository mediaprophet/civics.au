# Camping infrastructure: population scenarios and statistical evidence catalogue

Planning baseline: 25 September 2026. This catalogue defines potential users and the evidence needed to estimate local need, service fit and likely utilisation. It is an input to evaluation design, not a claim that every group is suited to camping, that an aggregate count represents demand, or that camping can replace secure housing or specialist support.

## Purpose and boundaries

The infrastructure may offer low-cost, lawful and dignified short-to-medium stay capacity; reliable connectivity; amenities; safe storage; transport links; work/study facilities; community connection; and a pathway to longer-term housing or a viable livelihood. Its most plausible role varies by person and location: temporary base, seasonal work accommodation, study-enabling site, recovery/stabilisation option, pilot/project workspace or resilience accommodation.

It is not a substitute for social housing, family violence refuge, hospital discharge planning, disability-accessible housing, child protection, immigration support or clinical care. A person can prefer a camping-based arrangement and still need safe, permanent housing and specialist support. A person can also be statistically within a cohort and have no wish or suitability to use a site.

Count people, households, places and episodes separately. One person may appear in several scenarios, and a service visit is not a unique person. The model must de-duplicate at case level only with consent and lawful authority; public statistics should remain aggregate and should never be combined to create a claimed total number of “eligible campers.”

## Evidence states

| State | Meaning in this catalogue |
|---|---|
| Direct count | A published series measures the specified population close to the scenario |
| Relevant proxy | A published series measures a related population or pressure, but does not show likely site use |
| Context only | A valid contextual series that cannot size the service population |
| Local primary research required | No appropriate public measure; use voluntary, consent-based research and service data |
| Not appropriate to count | The scenario concerns a sensitive preference, harm or private circumstance; do not derive a number from adjacent datasets |

Every imported observation needs geography, reference period, denominator, collection method, privacy/disclosure class and limitations. The ontology must carry these fields with the value.

## Cross-cutting international and public-visibility considerations

International status can apply to every pathway above. A student, artist, seasonal worker, entrepreneur, contributor or person taking a recovery break may be visiting, studying, working, settling or otherwise lawfully present from another country. Nationality, country of origin and visa status must never be used as a proxy for economic need, safety, character, local demand or eligibility. The operator should request only the information needed to meet a specific legal obligation, use an authorised support or migration service where needed, and offer information in accessible languages.

Use [Home Affairs visa statistics](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/research-and-statistics/statistics/visa-statistics), including its [temporary-entrants stock data](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/research-and-statistics/statistics/visa-statistics/live/temporary-entrants-in-australia), as aggregate programme context, and [ABS Overseas Arrivals and Departures](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/tourism-and-transport/overseas-arrivals-and-departures-australia) for movement context. These measure visa programmes, temporary entrants or movements; they do not establish a local population, a person's right to work or study, or likely use of the infrastructure.

Some people may also wish to take part without public attention because they are recognisable, have a public-facing role, or simply value anonymity. The site should make ordinary, low-profile participation available to everyone: minimal publication of participant information, no marketing based on identity, neutral booking/contact practices, and a proportionate person-led safety plan when a disclosed risk justifies it. Public recognition is not a privileged service tier and should not create influence over selection, governance or other participants.

## Common overlapping pressures

The planning model should allow several pressures to coexist without assigning blame or creating a risk score:

- Relationship breakdown combined with mortgage, vehicle, tax, consumer or business obligations.
- Study or seasonal work combined with high rental costs, distance, insecure employment or weak transport.
- Business closure combined with personal guarantees, income loss and a need to preserve employment capacity.
- Pregnancy/parenthood combined with housing insecurity, family separation, health needs or loss of income.
- Online harm, image-based abuse or coercive control combined with privacy, income, legal and housing challenges.
- Loss of income after raising concerns about, resisting or leaving alleged organisational wrongdoing, combined with professional isolation, legal costs, trauma and debt obligations.
- People with economic security seeking a meaningful way to make, build, mentor, care for place, share knowledge or participate in civic work, with transparent boundaries so resources do not become control over participant choices or public-interest decisions.
- Creative practice, event production or festival-season work combined with travel, irregular hours, equipment needs, rehearsal space, collaboration and a desire to contribute to cultural life. The model should value declared cultural and community outcomes in their own right, rather than treating revenue as their only benefit.
- A planned break from sustained high-pressure skilled work combined with a need for quiet, agency, connection and a bounded opportunity to do useful work. This is a voluntary recovery-and-contribution hypothesis, not a diagnosis, workforce-retention programme or substitute for clinical care, leave entitlements or safe housing.
- Disability, hospital/care exit, mental health or trauma combined with accessibility and ongoing-support needs.
- Disaster displacement combined with communications, insurance, work interruption and temporary service loss.

These intersections are hypotheses for service design and evaluation. They should be recorded as voluntary self-described circumstances, never inferred by joining public records. The model should evaluate obligation type, timing, affordability and housing options, not request unnecessary debt balances or sensitive personal histories.

## Minimum suitability and safeguarding screen

Before counting a scenario as serviceable capacity, test the actual site and pathway for:

1. Voluntary, informed choice; lawful tenure/visa conditions; no coercion or exclusion from better housing options.
2. Physical access, sanitation, water, power, connectivity, transport, heat/cold/fire/flood safety and disability access.
3. Privacy, child safety, safeguarding, conflict management, secure storage and information minimisation.
4. Family violence, trauma, health, disability, pregnancy/parenting, child-protection and other specialist referral routes.
5. Capacity for the promised length of stay, support intensity, exit pathway and no sudden displacement.
6. Affordability after transport, food, communications, debt obligations, childcare, utilities and other essential costs.
7. Clear complaints, review and participant representation mechanisms.

A failed or unresolved safeguard is a design constraint, not a discount to be offset by a financial benefit. This is particularly important for people escaping violence, children, people with significant care needs and anyone whose safety could be compromised by an open/shared setting.

## Local demand-estimation protocol

1. Select a site catchment, travel radius, capacity, service model and period. Record location and geographic boundary versions.
2. Import the direct counts and proxies above only for the selected geography/time period. Preserve denominators and caveats.
3. Obtain local evidence: anonymous voluntary survey, provider/employer interviews, education-provider evidence, seasonal roster/vacancy projections, site constraints and existing accommodation supply. Record sampling/frame/non-response.
4. Estimate an **addressable, suitable and voluntary** population separately for each scenario. Use explicit rates/ranges rather than a single total. Never sum overlapping scenarios without a documented overlap model.
5. Test travel distance, transport, accessibility, legal status, safety and willingness before forecasting occupancy. These constraints often reduce an apparently large contextual population sharply.
6. Model occupancy by month/week and length of stay, not only annual people. Include turn-away, seasonal peaks, no-shows and exit pathways.
7. Publish low/base/high cases with a source trace and switching-value analysis. Show which local research result would most change capacity or viability.
8. Pilot, observe actual use/outcomes, and compare with the original forecast without overwriting it.

## Research ethics and privacy rules

- Do not collect sexual history, reproductive intention, relationship conflict, debt balances, immigration status, health/disability information or harm experience merely to qualify someone for a campsite.
- Do not collect allegations, disclosure documents, employer identities, legal strategy, case outcomes or whistleblower status unless essential to a person-led support pathway and handled by an appropriate independent professional service. The camping operator should not investigate or adjudicate workplace wrongdoing.
- Do not infer wealth, philanthropic capacity or motivations from age, address, occupation, superannuation, taxation or public reputation. People offering resources choose what to disclose, under clear purposes and conflict-of-interest rules.
- Do not collect nationality, passport details, visa status, employer/security details, public profile or travel history unless a specific legal, safety or service purpose makes it necessary. Keep any such information restricted, time-limited and separate from aggregate evaluation graphs.
- For sensitive pathways, let people choose what to disclose and who can see it. Store purpose, consent, retention, sharing and withdrawal controls with any case evidence.
- Do not publish small cell counts or maps that could identify a person, refuge, minor, family-violence survivor, creator affected by image-based abuse or other vulnerable participant.
- Use specialist partners and participant reviewers to define safe questions, referral processes and whether a scenario should be served at all.
- Keep public planning statistics, restricted case information and any confidential operational evidence in separate graphs and Q42 volumes with explicit access classes.

## Decisions this catalogue can support

After the first packs are populated, the evaluation model can answer: what seasonal capacity is plausible; which user pathways are locally evidenced; which facility features and referral agreements are essential; whether the site is suitable for families/access needs; what financial/technical/social assumptions drive viability; and what pilot evidence is worth collecting next.

It cannot, by itself, determine a person’s housing suitability, safety, pregnancy decision, family circumstance, debt capacity or entitlement to assistance. Those are human, legal, service and consent questions requiring an appropriate pathway.

The purpose-led pathway is not an economic-refuge category and it is not a fundraising segment. It recognises that people who have enough material security may seek a life of usefulness, belonging and agency through making, mentoring, caring for place, sharing knowledge or doing patient civic work. Retirees and contributors with substantial resources may support a case, but must not receive informal control over participant data, resident selection, complaints, evaluation findings or public decisions in return. Contributions should be recorded as voluntary commitments or negotiated agreements with scope, conditions, conflicts, valuation and governance rights stated separately.

The creative-practice pathway likewise recognises artistic work, cultural participation and event contribution as forms of purpose. It is not a claim that artists require economic refuge, a substitute for fair pay, or a reason to convert culture into a financial proxy. A site can only claim cultural value after practitioners, neighbouring communities and event partners have defined the activity, safeguards, benefits and trade-offs; financial viability, fair remuneration and public cultural outcomes should remain separately visible in the evaluation.

The skilled-work recovery pathway is a choice to take time for rest, reflection and purposeful activity after demanding work; it must never become an informal assessment of a person's health, resilience, professional conduct or employability. Occupation data can describe the workforce context, but only confidential opt-in local research can establish whether people want this type of pathway. The service should define quiet, privacy, duration, safeguarding, contribution boundaries and independent clinical, employment and housing referrals before it claims a recovery benefit.

International participation and low-profile participation are cross-cutting design requirements. Aggregate visa and movement data may inform timing and broad context, but no public dataset can establish personal intent, lawful conditions, accommodation need or the desire to be known or unknown. The default should be to treat every participant as an ordinary person, with equal privacy, fair access and no identity-based marketing or governance privilege.
